1. Executive Summary

ACME currently manages salary information for 10,000 employees across multiple countries using Excel spreadsheets.

This creates challenges around:

Data consistency and accuracy.
Employee salary updates.
Historical salary tracking.
Compensation analysis.
Reporting and visibility.
Data security and accountability.

The proposed Salary Management Platform will centralize employee compensation information through a secure web application.

The platform will allow HR managers to manage employee salary information, track historical changes, generate reports, and understand how the organization compensates its workforce.

6. Out of Scope

The following features are deliberately excluded from the MVP:

Payroll processing and salary disbursement.
Tax calculations.
Attendance and leave management.
Insurance and employee benefits.
Automated salary recommendations.
Complex approval workflows.
External HRMS integrations.

Reason: These features introduce additional business complexity. The initial objective is to establish a reliable source of truth for employee compensation.

Database Design Decisions
Employee and salary data are separated.
Salary history is maintained using effective dates.
Monetary values use PostgreSQL DECIMAL.
Salary currency is explicitly stored.
Previous salary records are not overwritten during normal salary changes.
Salary changes and audit events are written transactionally.
Employee search and filtering columns are indexed.
Currency Consideration

ACME operates across multiple countries.

INR, USD, GBP, and EUR must not be added directly.

For consolidated reporting:

Maintain exchange rates.
Select a reporting currency.
Define an exchange-rate date.
Store or reproduce the conversion basis.
Clearly distinguish converted payroll from local payroll.

11. End-to-End User Journey
HR manager logs in.
Application displays the HR dashboard.
HR manager reviews employee and salary statistics.
HR manager navigates to employee directory.
Searches using employee ID, name, or department.
Opens an employee profile.
Reviews current salary and historical salary records.
Updates salary and enters an effective date and reason.
Backend validates the request.
Backend closes the previous effective salary period.
Backend creates the new salary record.
Backend creates the salary history and audit events.
Database transaction commits.
Frontend refreshes the employee profile.
HR manager can view the change in audit history.

22. Key Architectural Decisions
Use a modular monolith instead of microservices.
Separate employee data from compensation data.
Maintain effective-dated salary history.
Store monetary amounts using DECIMAL.
Preserve currency information.
Make salary changes transactional.
Maintain immutable audit events.
Enforce authorization at the backend.
Use server-side pagination and aggregation.
Prioritize business correctness over unnecessary infrastructure complexity.
Conclusion

The ACME Salary Management Platform should be designed as a secure, maintainable compensation management system rather than a basic employee CRUD application.

The most important engineering priorities are:

Correct salary domain modeling.
Accurate historical salary tracking.
Multi-currency support.
Secure compensation data access.
Transactional salary changes.
Meaningful HR analytics.
Reliable reporting.
Automated testing.

The recommended MVP architecture provides a strong foundation for future expansion into payroll integrations, approval workflows, compensation planning, and broader HR operations.

Primary Engineering Principle:

Treat employee compensation as sensitive, historically significant business data. Every salary change must be validated, traceable, and consistent.
